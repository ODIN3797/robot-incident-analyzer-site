Exercise 1 of 3 · DOCK

Docking success, charging error

Incident I004 · recorded 2026-10-10T10:00:47.000Z UTC · Finding “Charging issue after successful docking” (HIGH)

Start with E0009 at 2026-10-10T10:00:40.000Z (INFO, DOCK_SUCCESS): “Docking success; robot aligned.” Then inspect E0010 at 2026-10-10T10:00:47.000Z (ERROR, CHARGING): “Charging current is 0 A after docking.”

Look for: the order of the two messages, their timestamps, and the difference between a reported docking state and an independently verified physical charging state.

Ask yourself: which charger status, battery-current or contact-alignment evidence would you still need before calling this a charging fault?

Important: the report records that a docking-success log preceded a charging error. Physical alignment and charging contact are not independently verified, so the finding stays a candidate.

Exercise 2 of 3 · NAV

Communication warnings before a navigation abort

Incident I003 · recorded 2026-10-10T10:00:15.000Z → 2026-10-10T10:00:22.000Z UTC · Finding “Navigation failure follows another anomaly” (HIGH)

Read E0006 at 2026-10-10T10:00:15.000Z (WARN, COMMUNICATION): “Network packet loss started.” Then E0007 at 2026-10-10T10:00:18.000Z (ERROR, TIMEOUT): “MQTT request timeout after 3 retries.” Then E0008 at 2026-10-10T10:00:22.000Z (ERROR, NAVIGATION): “Navigation goal aborted by controller.”

Look for: the sequence and the spacing between the three recorded events, and which robot and node each one belongs to.

Ask yourself: which controller-state, clock or connectivity evidence would let you test whether the earlier warning is related at all?

Important: an earlier anomaly is not proof that it caused a later navigation failure. Timestamp proximity only puts them in the same place to look.

Exercise 3 of 3 · MOTION

Commanded velocity versus odometry

Incident I001 · recorded 2026-10-10T12:00:00.250Z → 2026-10-10T12:00:01.410Z UTC · Finding “Commanded velocity diverges from odometry” (MEDIUM)

The report links this finding to E0015 at 2026-10-10T12:00:00.310Z (WARN, CMD_ODOM_MISMATCH): “/cmd_vel: command 0.50 m/s vs /odom: odom 0.00 m/s for 0.30s. Check safety state, command source and frame/timing alignment.”

Look for: what was commanded, what the odometry recorded, and how long the two disagreed — then read the follow-up checks printed with the finding.

Ask yourself: was motion enabled, were units and timestamps aligned, and is the robot's actual physical movement known from this recording at all?

Important: these observations do not establish a verified mechanical or software root cause. The finding is rated MEDIUM and its evidence status is “direct observed signal / root cause not verified”.

Adjacent context: E0014 at 2026-10-10T12:00:00.250Z (WARN, TF_MAP_ODOM_JUMP) records “map -> odom moved 1.50 m over 0.200 s; may reflect localization correction.” It sits in the same incident window as E0015, but the report does not list it as direct evidence for this finding and it is not proof that the transform change caused the velocity mismatch.

What this sample cannot establish
